# Planning a Business Website

Welcome to WebCrafts Guides. This documentation helps owners, founders, and marketing teams plan a business website before they brief a developer or an agency. A short planning stage saves money later: it turns a vague idea into a clear scope, a realistic budget, and a launch date the whole team can work towards.

Use this page as your starting point. It explains how to define what your site needs to do, how website pricing is usually structured, which costs keep recurring after launch, and how to compare quotes on equal terms.

## Start with goals and scope

Begin with the job the website has to do. For most small and mid-sized businesses that means generating enquiries, explaining services clearly, and building trust with proof such as testimonials, case studies, and certifications. Write down the pages you need on day one, the pages that can wait, and any features that affect price, such as online booking, payments, a customer login, or a product catalogue.

A one-page scope note is enough at this stage. List your audience, your three most important pages, the actions visitors should take, and examples of websites you like. Developers quote far more accurately from a written scope than from a phone call, and a shared scope makes competing quotes comparable.

## Understand the budget

Website quotes vary widely because scope, design effort, and content readiness vary. A template-based brochure site, a custom-designed marketing site, and an e-commerce store are three different projects with three different price bands, so the first step is knowing which category your project falls into.

## What affects the price

- **Number of pages and layouts** — unique templates take longer to design and build than repeated ones.
- **Design approach** — a ready theme is cheaper; a custom design tailored to your brand costs more.
- **Features** — forms, booking, payments, multilingual content, and integrations all add development time.
- **Content readiness** — writing, photography, and product data supplied late are a common cause of delays.
- **Revisions** — agree on how many review rounds are included before work starts.

## Plan for running costs

The build fee is only part of the total cost of owning a website. Budget each year for domain renewal, hosting, an SSL certificate (often bundled with hosting), software or plugin licences, routine maintenance and backups, and small content updates. Keeping a modest annual maintenance budget avoids the far larger cost of rescuing an outdated or compromised site.

## A simple launch checklist

1. Confirm the scope, pages, and features in writing.
2. Register the domain in your own business account.
3. Approve the design on mobile first, where most visitors will see it.
4. Supply final text and images before the build starts.
5. Test every form and the checkout or booking flow end to end.
6. Set up analytics and search console access under your own accounts.
7. Agree on who maintains the site after launch, and at what cost.

## Next steps

Once your scope note is ready, shortlist two or three developers, send each of them the same brief, and compare the itemised quotes against the checklist above. A clear plan, a realistic budget, and written ownership of your domain and accounts will keep the project on track from kickoff to launch.
